#### **Motor Drivers**



**1. L298N**

Driver IC: STMicroelectronics L298N dual H-bridge chip.

Motor Voltage Range (Vs): 5V to 35V 

Output Current: Up to 2A peak current per channel/bridge (4A total)

Speed \& Direction Control: Uses PWM (Pulse Width Modulation) via ENA/ENB pins for speed, and IN1–IN4 logic pins for rotational direction.

Supported Motors: Normal BO



**2. L293D Motor Driver shield**

Driver Chips: Dual L293D H-bridge chips

Output Current: 0.6A per channel (1.2A peak)

Motor Voltage Range: 4.5V to 36V

Supported Motors:

• Up to 4 bi-directional DC motors with 8-bit speed selection

• Up to 2 stepper motors (unipolar or bipolar)

• Up to 2 5V hobby servo motors



#### **Motor and it's Types**



1. **Bidirectional BO Motors**

A lightweight, low-cost electric motor equipped with a built-in plastic gearbox to reduce speed and increase turning power (torque)

A BO motor just spins continuously when connected to a battery



2\. **Stepper Motor**

A stepper motor is an electric motor that moves in precise, individual increments called "steps."

&#x20;

\- Unipolar(5 - 6 Wires)

Unipolar Stepper Motors have an extra center connection (called a center tap) on each coil, allowing electricity to flow in only one direction. They are easier to control but have less power. 

&#x20;               

\- Bipolar(4 Wires)

Bipolar Stepper Motors use the entire coil without a center tap, requiring electricity to reverse directions. They are harder to control but have much more power.



3\. **Servo Motor** 

A servo motor is an electric motor that can turn to and hold a specific, precise angle or position. 

Unlike a regular motor that spins continuously when you plug it in, a servo motor can be told exactly where to stop
